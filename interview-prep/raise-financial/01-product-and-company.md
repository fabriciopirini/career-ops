# Raise Financial: Product and Company

Research cutoff: 2026-08-02. This brief separates Raise's own descriptions from independent facts and does not make a securities, tax, or regulatory conclusion.

## The 60 second explanation

Raise Investment is a paid membership that gives a customer the economic upside from an S&P 500 linked position funded with capital that Raise says comes from institutional partners. In the current pilot described by its FAQ, the customer pays $30 per month and Raise allocates $5,000 of principal. The payment is Raise revenue, not a customer contribution to the investment. Raise says the customer is entitled to gains above the original principal, while the principal remains Raise's or its partners' and is returned when the customer exits. Early principal exposure sits in a defined outcome, buffered ETF with a 12 month outcome window, a downside buffer, and an upside cap. Gains above the protected principal are described as moving into uncapped S&P 500 exposure. The customer can cancel, but cannot make a partial withdrawal: exit means selling the position, returning principal, and sending the customer's take-home value to a linked bank account. Raise says there is no customer loan, margin debt, interest charge, performance fee, or exit fee. Returns are not guaranteed, the buffer has conditions, and the public material does not explain the legal account ownership, exact ETF, cap, buffer, custody, loss allocation, or tax reporting. ([How it works](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10509287-how-does-raise-investment-work), [capital source](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508658-where-does-raise-investment-get-its-investment-capital), [exit FAQ](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508915-how-do-i-cash-out-or-exit-my-raise-investment-membership), accessed 2026-08-02)

### The customer problem, without the sales pitch

Raise targets people who want greater long-term market exposure but have limited monthly cash after housing, debt, childcare, or other costs. Its proposed trade is earlier exposure to a larger principal in exchange for a recurring fee and capped upside on the protected portion. This is not the same as investing the membership payment. The company argues that earlier lump sum exposure can compound faster than investing the same monthly cash flow gradually, but public projections rely on assumed market returns and long holding periods and are not promised outcomes. ([Product video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlcBSZJRyQY), [revenue FAQ](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508670-how-does-raise-investment-make-money-what-s-the-catch), accessed 2026-08-02)

## Contradictions and unclear language

1. **"Never below" conflicts with the fuller risk explanation.** The asset FAQ says the buffer prevents the asset falling below its initial purchase price, and the video calls it a guarantee. Other FAQs say the buffer absorbs only part of a decline, applies as stated only over a full window, can differ on mid-period exit, and does not cover losses beyond its limit. The accurate shorthand is targeted downside buffering with conditions, not guaranteed principal. ([Assets](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10504567-what-assets-is-raise-investment-actually-invested-in), [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlcBSZJRyQY), [downturn](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10509139-what-happens-when-the-market-takes-a-downturn), accessed 2026-08-02)
2. **"Our capital" and partner capital are interchangeable in copy.** Raise says it fronts "our" money but also says partners advance and recover it. The funding chain and loss bearer are not public. ([Mechanics](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10509287-how-does-raise-investment-work), [capital](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508658-where-does-raise-investment-get-its-investment-capital), accessed 2026-08-02)
3. **"100% of returns" is less precise than gains above principal.** Copy alternates among returns, gains, growth, and take-home value without defining dividends, fund costs, realized losses, taxes, or high-water marks. ([Value](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10513578-for-raise-investment-what-s-the-difference-between-the-market-value-and-the-take-home-value), [dividends](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10509089-does-raise-investment-provide-dividends), accessed 2026-08-02)
4. **Exit at any time does not mean the stated buffer applies at any time.** A mid-period sale can differ from the stated cap and buffer. ([Exit](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508915-how-do-i-cash-out-or-exit-my-raise-investment-membership), accessed 2026-08-02)
5. **Pilot, beta, waitlist, and planned public release coexist.** Public sources do not establish who can open an account today. ([Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlcBSZJRyQY), [seed announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/raise-financial-closes-2-million-seed-round-jumpstart-wealth-3ovcf/), accessed 2026-08-02)
6. **Public product information lags Patrick's pivot.** The help center still lists Raise Investment and Raise Education. Patrick told Fabricio that Raise is focusing only on Investment and retiring the education surface. Treat that as current interview context, not a publicly documented launch. ([Company FAQ](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10514129-what-is-raise-financial), accessed 2026-08-02)

## Customer lifecycle

### Confirmed flow

1. **Request access.** Public sources variously describe signup, limited beta, a waitlist, and a planned broader release. ([Video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TlcBSZJRyQY), [seed announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/raise-financial-closes-2-million-seed-round-jumpstart-wealth-3ovcf/), accessed 2026-08-02)
2. **Create a Raise account.** From the dashboard, select "Create an account" and "Investment Account." ([Signup](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10887291-how-do-i-create-a-raise-investment-account), accessed 2026-08-02)
3. **Provide details.** Supply demographics, indicate an account level, and schedule an onboarding call. ([Signup](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10887291-how-do-i-create-a-raise-investment-account), accessed 2026-08-02)
4. **Complete onboarding.** Raise asks about goals, explains the product, decides if it is a good fit, and approves the account. ([Signup](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10887291-how-do-i-create-a-raise-investment-account), accessed 2026-08-02)
5. **Open the position.** Raise says it typically makes the upfront investment within 24 to 72 hours after setup. The pilot maps $30 monthly to $5,000 principal. ([Signup](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10887291-how-do-i-create-a-raise-investment-account), [pricing](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508892-how-much-does-raise-investment-cost), accessed 2026-08-02)
6. **Maintain membership.** Raise collects the recurring payment. The customer sees market and take-home values. Outcome windows reset automatically, and value above protected principal is described as moving to uncapped exposure. ([Mechanics](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10509287-how-does-raise-investment-work), [value](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10513578-for-raise-investment-what-s-the-difference-between-the-market-value-and-the-take-home-value), accessed 2026-08-02)
7. **Adjust or exit.** Raise says the customer may change tier or exit, although beta has one tier. Exit requires support contact. Raise sells, returns principal to partners, transfers take-home value to the linked bank, and ends membership. ([Adjustment](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508905-how-do-i-adjust-my-raise-investment-membership), [exit](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10508915-how-do-i-cash-out-or-exit-my-raise-investment-membership), accessed 2026-08-02)

### Lifecycle gaps that matter

* Waitlist conversion, invitation expiry, identity verification, eligibility rules, agreements, rejection, and appeal.
* Bank linking, payment authorization, first charge, failed payment, grace period, refunds, and chargebacks.
* Brokerage ownership, required disclosures, and evidence of consent.
* Order price and timing, partial fills, outcome window assignment, rebalancing, and market holidays.
* Audit rules for market value, take-home value, gains, fees, and tax lots.
* Notices for cap and buffer resets, loss, payment failure, liquidation, or restriction.
* Statements, confirmations, tax forms, complaints, beneficiaries, death, incapacity, and fraud.
* Exit quote, cancellation cutoff, settlement failures, post-exit records, and data retention.

### Interview-supplied pivot

Patrick told Fabricio that Raise is focusing exclusively on Raise Investment and retiring the education product surface. He framed immediate work as a focused mobile build, followed later by internal capital management and operations tooling. This is the strongest current interview signal, but is not yet reflected in the public FAQ, which still lists Raise Education. A key follow-up is whether the pivot changes servicing obligations for existing education accounts. ([Public company FAQ](https://help.raisefinancial.com/en/articles/10514129-what-is-raise-financial), accessed 2026-08-02)

## Questions for Patrick, ordered by interview value

1. **What is the exact legal and operational account model?** Who owns the brokerage account and ETF shares, what grants the member gains, who is custodian, and what happens if Raise or a partner fails?
2. **Which invariants may never break in the mobile cutover?** Principal attribution, take-home value, membership, trades, outcome window, payout eligibility, and audit history are likely candidates.
3. **How is take-home value calculated?** Which prices, expenses, dividends, taxes, realized losses, pending trades, and rounding rules enter it, and which system is authoritative?
4. **What exact ETF and outcome process are used?** Ticker, cap, buffer, dates, expense ratio, mid-window enrollment, annual reset, and movement into uncapped exposure.
5. **Who bears a principal shortfall?** If the position falls below advanced principal, especially after mid-period exit or beyond-buffer loss, who absorbs it and can member gains be netted against it?
6. **What is target unit economics by tier?** How do revenue, capital partner cost, ETF cap, fund and payment costs, servicing, duration, churn, and capital utilization fit together?
7. **What triggers an involuntary liquidation?** The FAQ mentions "other liquidation events." What are they, what notice is given, and how are gains and fees handled?
8. **What is live today?** Paying accounts, tiers, states, capital capacity, active waitlist, and launch criteria.
9. **What did the pilot change?** Beyond reported zero churn, what was learned about comprehension, support, payments, exits, loss reactions, and conversion?
10. **What does "good fit" mean?** Which eligibility or suitability rules are deterministic, which need human judgment, and what evidence must be retained?
11. **What happens after payment failure?** When does entitlement stop, is there a grace period, do accrued gains remain, and how do refunds or chargebacks affect the position?
12. **What promise should the app make about protection?** How will Raise reconcile "never below" with mid-period pricing, finite buffers, beyond-buffer loss, and resets?
13. **How do tier changes work?** Does a change open a lot or outcome window, alter principal, create a taxable event, or require partner approval?
14. **Who issues statements and tax forms?** Which party reports what, when does holding begin, and how are membership payments treated?
15. **What remains for Raise Education?** Which servicing and record obligations survive, who owns support, and what can safely be retired?
16. **What milestone does the seed fund?** Public launch, activated members, retained recurring revenue, committed capital, automation, or the next financing case?
