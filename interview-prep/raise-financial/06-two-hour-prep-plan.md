# Two-Hour Preparation Plan

## Goal

Finish with five things you can say without notes:

1. A correct 60 second explanation of Raise Investment.
2. A concise explanation of why you fit despite the Expo and Alpaca gaps.
3. A defensible approach to the two-month launch.
4. Three stories with clear results and honest boundaries.
5. Four questions that help Patrick picture you on the team.

Do not try to learn the entire stack. Learn the product states, the riskiest boundaries, and the language needed to ask good questions.

## Full plan

### 0:00 to 0:25: Understand the product

Read `01-product-and-company.md`.

Then explain aloud, without looking:

1. Who the product is for.
2. Who supplies the initial capital.
3. What the member pays.
4. What the member does and does not own.
5. The difference between Market Value and Take-Home Value.
6. Why a Buffered ETF has both a buffer and a cap.
7. What happens during an Exit.

Open `CONTEXT.md` only when a term is unclear. Do not memorize marketing copy.

**Checkpoint:** You can explain the product without calling it a savings account, loan, ordinary brokerage account, or guaranteed return.

### 0:25 to 0:50: Learn the risky technical boundaries

Read the executive summary and risk sections of `02-stack-and-delivery.md`.

Focus on:

1. What can be shared across iOS, Android, and web.
2. Where native behavior, secure storage, provider SDKs, and app review create separate constraints.
3. Alpaca account, transfer, event, and sandbox concepts.
4. Webhook replay, idempotency, reconciliation, and source-of-truth decisions.
5. The difference between product analytics, logs, and an auditable financial event history.
6. Why existing backend behavior should be characterized before reuse.

**Checkpoint:** You can name the first three technical unknowns you would investigate without prescribing an architecture you have not seen.

### 0:50 to 1:05: Learn Patrick's operating language

Read `03-patrick-sdlc-and-signals.md`.

Write down three points where his public workflow matches how you already work. Choose one real example for each:

1. Planning before implementation.
2. Small, reviewable phases with human ownership.
3. Verification against actual behavior before release.

**Checkpoint:** You can explain your AI workflow without saying only that you use Cursor, Codex, or Pi.

### 1:05 to 1:25: Tighten your positioning

Read these sections in `04-positioning-and-stories.md`:

1. The position to take.
2. 60 second introduction.
3. Technical gap answer pattern.
4. Founding engineer judgment.

Say the introduction once. Then answer the React Native, Expo, Alpaca, and backend questions aloud.

**Checkpoint:** Every gap answer has evidence, a boundary, transferable knowledge, and a first action.

### 1:25 to 1:45: Prepare stories and questions

Choose three stories from `04-positioning-and-stories.md`:

1. React Native support portal.
2. Five storefronts and delivery process.
3. Either VRT infrastructure or the microservices refactor, depending on whether you need stronger quality or backend evidence.

For each story, remember only:

1. The problem.
2. Your decision.
3. The observable result.
4. The boundary or lesson.

Then read `05-call-plan.md` and choose four primary questions plus two backups.

**Checkpoint:** Each story is under 90 seconds and each question has a reason you care about the answer.

### 1:45 to 2:00: Rehearse the call

Use `07-rehearsal.md`.

Answer these five questions aloud:

1. Tell me about yourself and why Raise.
2. What exactly did you own in React Native?
3. How will you ramp up on Expo and Alpaca?
4. How would you ship this in under two months?
5. What questions do you have for me?

Finish with your closing sentence from `05-call-plan.md`.

Do one pass only. Fix answers that are inaccurate or longer than two minutes. Leave natural wording alone.

## 45-minute minimum plan

If time collapses, use this order:

1. **12 minutes:** Read the product summary and lifecycle in `01-product-and-company.md`.
2. **8 minutes:** Review `CONTEXT.md` and explain Market Value, Take-Home Value, Advanced Principal, Buffer, Cap, and Exit.
3. **10 minutes:** Read the top risks and Expo or Alpaca primers in `02-stack-and-delivery.md`.
4. **8 minutes:** Practice the introduction and technical gap pattern in `04-positioning-and-stories.md`.
5. **7 minutes:** Choose four questions from `05-call-plan.md` and rehearse the close.

Skip broad background reading. Product accuracy and honest technical positioning matter more.

## Ten minutes immediately before the call

1. Put `05-call-plan.md` on screen.
2. Keep only four questions visible.
3. Say your introduction once, slowly.
4. Re-read the React Native and Alpaca boundaries.
5. Check audio, camera, notifications, and the meeting link.
6. Take one minute without reading anything.

## After the call

Within 15 minutes, write down:

1. Patrick's definition of success for the first 30 and 60 days.
2. Confirmed launch scope and explicit exclusions.
3. The legal and operational ownership of the Position, Advanced Principal, and Take-Home Value.
4. Which backend behavior is genuinely reusable.
5. Who owns product, capital markets rules, design, and provider relationships.
6. The next interview step and any preparation requested.
7. A reminder to Patrick about the OpenAI project engineer introductions, as he requested.

Send a short thank-you note that references one concrete point from the discussion. Do not send a generic recap of your resume.
